Why Garage Doors Fail More Often in Sudbury

Sudbury's climate is one of the most demanding in Ontario for garage door components. Temperatures regularly drop below -25°C in winter, and the freeze-thaw cycles that come with the shoulder seasons are particularly hard on torsion springs, cables, and weather seals. Cold metal becomes brittle — springs that are already under high tension snap under thermal stress. Rubber weather seals crack and lose their seal. Cables corrode from road salt tracked into the garage. These aren't unusual problems for Sudbury homeowners; they're predictable failures that our technicians see every week throughout the Greater Sudbury area.

The Most Common Garage Door Problems We Fix in Sudbury

Across our service calls in Sudbury, Hanmer, Chelmsford, Val Caron, and Capreol, these are the issues we see most often:

  • Broken torsion or extension springs — The single most common garage door failure. Springs are under extreme tension and typically last 7–10 years. Cold weather accelerates wear. We replace springs in pairs with high-cycle units rated for 20,000+ open-close cycles.
  • Snapped or frayed lift cables — Cables work alongside springs to balance the door's weight. A broken cable causes the door to hang unevenly or drop suddenly, which is a serious safety risk. We replace both cables simultaneously so the system stays balanced.
  • Garage door opener failure — Logic boards burn out, drive gears strip, and sensors fall out of alignment. We repair and replace all major opener brands including LiftMaster, Chamberlain, Genie, and Craftsman.
  • Door off track — Usually caused by a broken spring or cable, or by a vehicle impact. An off-track door should not be forced open or closed — call us for same-day realignment.
  • Bent or damaged panels — From vehicle impacts or storm damage. We can replace individual panels on most door models without replacing the entire door.
  • Worn or cracked weather seals — Critical in Sudbury winters. A failed bottom seal lets cold air, water, snow, and pests into the garage. We replace all seal types — bottom, sides, and top.

What to Do When Your Garage Door Won't Open

If your garage door stops working, here's what to check before calling: First, make sure the opener is plugged in and the circuit breaker hasn't tripped. Check the photo-eye sensors near the bottom of the door frame — if they're misaligned or dirty, the door won't close. If you can hear the motor running but the door isn't moving, you likely have a broken spring or disconnected trolley. If the door is off its tracks or you hear a loud bang followed by the door not working, stop operating it immediately — you likely have a broken spring and forcing it can cause further damage or injury. Garage Door Maintenance Tips for Sudbury Homeowners

To extend the life of your garage door and avoid costly repairs, Sudbury homeowners should lubricate hinges, rollers, and the torsion spring with a silicone-based spray every fall before temperatures drop. Test the door's auto-reverse safety feature monthly by placing a 2x4 flat on the ground in the door's path — it should reverse immediately on contact. Inspect the bottom weather seal before winter and replace it if it's cracked or hardened. If the door seems loud or shakes when operating, have a technician inspect the spring tension before it fails completely.
